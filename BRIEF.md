# The Lantern That Remembered: microsite brief

Self-authored under explicit creative delegation. The originating chat passed on
the owner's request ("an atmospheric responsive literary microsite ... full
unaltered story chapters, a character gallery, and a downloadable copy of the
Word document", keeping the copper / sea-glass / violet direction). No interview
was held; every decision below is mine, marked as authored.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe.** Salt-white daylight with something wrong in it. Moonlit maritime
   wonder, tactile ink, luminous sea glass (from design/style.json). References:
   an annotated Admiralty chart, a Studio Ghibli harbour at low tide, the
   endpapers of an old illustrated book.
2. **Journey.** Title page on the salt plain with the lighthouse beam sweeping
   in daylight. Chapter one in the town. A map you can hold a lantern over.
   Chapter two inside the lighthouse, on a dark ground. Chapter three, where the
   sea comes back across the same map. The three characters as plates. A
   colophon with the Word file.
3. **Energy.** Quiet open, quiet reading, a small private spark at the map,
   pressure in the lighthouse, one swell at the beam, then stillness.
4. **Feeling, and the one moment.** See the curve below. The moment: the copper
   coastline drawing itself and the sea filling back in under your scroll.
5. **The one thing no other site does.** Your pointer (or finger) is Tavi's
   lantern. Over Mira's redrawn map of fields and railway, its light shows the
   harbour that was erased. In chapter three the light stops being yours to
   hold: the shore draws itself in for good.
6. **Range.** Editorial, literary. Not premium-minimal marketing.
7. **Unbroken world or scenes.** Distinct chapters, hard cuts between grounds.
8. **Assets.** story.docx, three painted portraits (mira, tavi, keeper), the
   design system. Upgraded portraits and a wide hero may follow from Grok into
   artifacts/the-lantern-that-remembered/grok-images; the page is built to take
   them by swapping files.

## Grammar

Chaptered editorial. A story is read, not watched; chapters are the unit;
grounds change hard at each chapter. Folio in the margin, no fixed bar, title
page hero, colophon close. Filmic one-shot, live surface, continuous world,
typographic poster, gallery, split stage and cutlist all describe a different
object than a 939-word story.

Authored departure: the title page carries drawn (SVG/CSS) depth planes, no
raster media, so it stays a title page while meeting the layered-hero standard.

## Feeling curve

| Act | Feeling | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Title page | Unease, quiet wonder | Pale salt plain, a lighthouse beam sweeping in daylight, title set between sky and salt crust |
| Chapter 1 | Recognition, small dread | Paper ground, the story's plain sentences, Mira's portrait in the margin |
| The map | Private discovery | Your light shows the harbour under the fields |
| Chapter 2 | Pressure, grief | Hard cut to a violet-ink ground; the keeper's portrait drifting in its column |
| The beam (peak) | Release | Pinned map: copper coast draws, sea-glass fills, small lights travel home |
| Chapter 3 close of text | Tenderness | Back to quiet prose on a dawn ground |
| The three | Warmth | Character plates |
| Colophon | Rest, a light left on | Small type, the Word file, one lit lantern |

**Peak:** "You scroll and the sea literally comes back across the map." Lives in
chapter three at "The beam struck the town." Largest span on the page.

**Tell-someone sentence:** It's the site where you hold a lantern over a map and
see the harbour a whole town forgot.

**Authored silence:** the empty beat before the peak plate (one held line of
ground, no copy) is intentional.
